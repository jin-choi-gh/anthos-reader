# Concept

## Synopsis

A poem arrives in `text-data/` as a loom textual complex. [`loom/`](src/loom/) builds it into **Literary Material**: the poem's lines, each with loom's id for it (its [`Substrate`](src/material/types.ts)), its words, as loom divides them, and a [`Poem`](src/material/types.ts) of stanzas and lines. loom's own annotations are not read: the editor's notes are written in the practice.

An editor makes a [`Practice`](src/practice/types.ts) for that text in **Form Practice**: the **Practice Reel**. It is split into [`Stage`](src/practice/types.ts)s, and each stage into [`Step`](src/practice/types.ts)s, each named by an id that never changes (`stage-1`, `step-4`), and a step is written as its **parts**: words for the praxis bar (`prose`), which every step has, and at most one of these: the editor's notes to disclose one by one, in the step's order, each picked out in the poem as it is read (`disclose`), something for the reader to write (`writing`), the reader's own notes to bring back (`recall`) from an earlier stage or step that asked for writing, or words or lines of the poem to take out and give the reader to put back (`restore`): each taken out is a **gap**, and each piece given back a **slip**. Each [`EditorsNote`](src/practice/types.ts) is written in the step that shows it, and placed on the poem by its **notehead**, in loom's notation: `"l4 fellow && l4 raw"`. A practice is kept as a **practice file** in `practice-data/`, or saved in the browser from Form Practice. After each stage comes a **rest**, and after the last stage the **final stage**, which brings back everything the reader wrote in the practice, when it asked for any writing; the editor writes neither.

The **Practice Runner** takes one reader through the practice, over days. What the reader does is kept in a **log** of [`Event`](src/runner/types.ts)s: a step visited, a stage finished, a step's gaps restored, a step's notes disclosed. Replaying the log, with the reader's notebook and today's [`Day`](src/clock/clock.ts), gives the **standing** ([`Standing`](src/runner/types.ts)): the **station** (which stage, which step), the day the next stage opens, and the step the reader is **held** at. Reaching a stage's rest finishes it, once: going back to its steps afterwards is only to look again, and reaching the rest again finishes nothing. The first stage is open from the start; each later stage opens as many days after the reader finishes the one before as its **days** say, and the final stage as soon as the last is finished; the day it opens is fixed when the stage before is finished; a day starts at 04:00. The final stage stands at the last stage's rest: from it the reader may go back into the last stage, and reaching that rest again returns them to the final stage. A step that asks for writing holds the reader until the **User Notebook** has a note from it, written on this version of the practice. A step that restores holds the reader until every gap is restored, and until then they may not leave it at all: not back, not on, and not into annotate mode. A step that discloses holds the reader until every note is disclosed: they may go back, but not on; and since nothing is logged until the last note is disclosed, a step left before then is disclosed from its first note when they come back. The log is kept per version of the practice: a practice whose steps are edited is a new run. So the poem is not read all at once: it opens at the reader's pace, a day at a time, and each stage walks the reader through its steps.

On each step, the **Frame** takes the step the runner's **gate** holds ([`gateOf`](src/runner/gate.ts)), places its editor's notes on the poem by their noteheads, resolves what its writing asks to be written about against the poem, and finds what the reader has already written there. That is the **subject**. The **Scene** plays the subject: it keeps what is open and what is being written, and projects it to the **Viewer**, which draws the poem and the notes it picks out, or its gaps, and to the **Praxis Bar**, which draws the prose, the writing, the notes disclosed or the slips, and the frame's Back and Next. What the reader writes leaves the scene as an **effect**, and is kept in the notebook as the reader's own annotation, with where in the run it was written; a step whose gaps are all restored, or whose notes are all disclosed, leaves it as an effect too, and is kept in the run's log.

The same poem can also be read in **annotate mode**, with no runner: the notes the reader writes there, in the order written, which they can delete, and a note to write on passages they choose.

**Form Practice** is a screen of its own, in development only, opened from the library beside each poem. There a practice is built stage by stage and step by step, part by part; each editor's note is written on passages chosen in the poem, as in annotate mode. The practice being made, the **draft**, is kept in the browser until it is saved or discarded. A practice with no problems is saved in the browser, and can be exported as a practice file.

## Layers

### Literary Material: [`material/`](src/material/)

The material is the text as everything else uses it, and it knows nothing about any of them. [`toMaterial`](src/loom/toMaterial.ts) builds a [`Material`](src/material/materials.ts) from loom's JSON, with [`substrateOf`](src/loom/textualComplex.ts), [`toWords`](src/loom/toWords.ts) and [`toPoem`](src/loom/toPoem.ts), and refuses a poem with no text, or with no display or word-tokens schema. It does not read loom's annotations.

The **words** are the poem's words as loom's `word-tokens` schema divides them: each a span in one line, with no punctuation at either end, in the poem's order. loom makes that schema with every display schema, so a text with none is refused; a word loom gives that does not keep the rule for spans, or that overlaps the word before it, is left out.

The **substrate** ([`Substrate`](src/material/types.ts)) is the poem's lines, as loom keeps them, each with loom's id for it (`l4`), and the **fingerprint** ([`Fingerprint`](src/material/types.ts), made by [`fingerprintOf`](src/material/fingerprint.ts)) is a hash of those lines' text, not their ids, made with [`hashOf`](src/material/fingerprint.ts), the hash behind every fingerprint. The [`Poem`](src/material/types.ts) and its [`Line`](src/material/types.ts)s are the lines arranged for display. A text is known by its [`TextId`](src/material/types.ts), the file it comes from.

A **position** ([`Position`](src/material/types.ts)) is a line of the substrate and an offset in it, counted in code points ([`codePoints`](src/material/codePoints.ts)), the unit loom counts in; a space is what loom counts as one ([`isSpace`](src/material/codePoints.ts)). A **span** ([`Span`](src/material/types.ts)) runs from one position up to, not including, another, and follows loom's rule ([`spanProblem`](src/material/spans.ts)): it lies in the poem, runs forwards, never starts at a line's end and never ends at a line's start, so each stretch of text has one span. [`wholePoem`](src/material/spans.ts) is the span from the first line with text to the end of the last; [`sameSpan`](src/material/spans.ts) compares two spans, [`textOf`](src/material/spans.ts) cuts a span's text out of the substrate, and [`lineLength`](src/material/spans.ts) measures a line. [`comparePositions`](src/material/spans.ts) orders two positions; [`trimmedSpan`](src/material/spans.ts) makes a span between two positions that begins and ends on a character that is not a space, so that loom can always write it; [`wordAt`](src/material/spans.ts) is the word (a run of non-space characters) at a position; [`joinSpan`](src/material/spans.ts) adds a span to others, joining it with any it overlaps or touches; [`lineRangesOf`](src/material/spans.ts) gives a span's stretch on each line.

A **notehead** is loom's notation for what a note is about: targets separated by `&&`, each one locator or two joined by `~`, a locator being a line's id, then perhaps a quote from that line, then perhaps `[n]` for the quote's nth occurrence: `"l2 He ~ l3 halves"`, `"l1 ever[2]"`, `"l13"`. [`spansOfNotehead`](src/material/noteheads.ts) places a notehead on the substrate as loom does, or says why it cannot, refusing besides what loom could not read back as a head in a notes file and what breaks the rule for spans; [`noteheadOf`](src/material/noteheads.ts) writes spans as a notehead, so that placing it again gives the same spans. Quotes are matched in NFC and counted in code points, as loom counts them.

An **annotation** ([`Annotation`](src/material/types.ts)) is a note on the poem, as loom has one: an id, its **targets** (spans), its tags and its body. [`notesFor`](src/material/notes.ts) derives how annotations are shown, the editor's or the reader's: each [`Note`](src/material/types.ts) with its id, its lemma ([`lemmaOf`](src/material/notes.ts)) and its paragraphs, and each line's [`NoteRange`](src/material/types.ts)s; a note on the whole poem has none. Its [`Notes`](src/material/types.ts) are what the viewer is given.

### Practice Reel: [`practice/`](src/practice/)

A practice is written before it is run, and does not change while it runs: every field is `readonly`. A [`Step`](src/practice/types.ts) is its [`Parts`](src/practice/types.ts), under an id that names it ([`StepId`](src/practice/types.ts)), and nothing reads it any other way:

```ts
export interface Parts {
  readonly prose: readonly string[]
  readonly disclose: readonly EditorsNote[]
  readonly writing: Writing | null
  readonly recall: Recall | null
  readonly restore: Restore | null
}
```

A [`Stage`](src/practice/types.ts) has an id ([`StageId`](src/practice/types.ts)), a name, its steps, and its **days**: how many days after the reader finishes the stage before it opens, at least 1, or `null` on the first stage, which is open from the start. Stages and steps are found by their ids with [`stageIndexOf`](src/practice/places.ts) and [`stepPlaceOf`](src/practice/places.ts). An [`EditorsNote`](src/practice/types.ts) is a note as loom has one, written in the step that shows it: an id, a notehead, tags and a body; a step's notes are disclosed in the order the step gives them, which the editor sets. Every id in a practice, of a stage, a step or a note, is letters, digits, `-` and `_`, as loom allows. The rest and the final stage are written in the same terms, as [`REST`](src/practice/parts.ts) and [`finalPartsOf`](src/practice/parts.ts), and have no ids. A [`Recall`](src/practice/types.ts) names what it brings back: an earlier stage (`"stage"`), with the notes written in it, or an earlier step (`"step"`), with the notes written on it; only the final stage brings back the whole practice (`"all"`), when any step of the practice asks for writing, and nothing otherwise. A [`Restore`](src/practice/types.ts) says whether it takes out words or lines (`of`), for the whole step, and names its gaps, each by a notehead with one target: one of the poem's words (`"l3 Angle"`), or a whole line (`"l8"`); a gap is checked by where its notehead places it, however it is written. [`problemsOf`](src/practice/problems.ts) checks a practice against its text's substrate and words before it runs, and gives each [`Problem`](src/practice/problems.ts) with its place (its stage, step and note) and what it is about; [`wordedProblem`](src/practice/problems.ts) puts one into words. It refuses: no stages; an empty title; a stage with an empty name, or no steps; an id that is malformed, or that another stage, step or note, of the same kind, has too; days on the first stage, or days on a later one that are not a whole number of at least 1; a step with no prose; a step with more than one of disclose, writing, recall and restore, since a step asks one thing of the reader and a subject has one commentary; a note with an empty body, or a notehead that cannot be placed; and a recall of the whole practice, or one that names a stage or step that is not there, that does not come before it (a stage must be an earlier stage; a step may be earlier in the same stage), or that asks for nothing to be written: a stage none of whose steps asks for writing, or a step that does not; and a restore with fewer than two gaps, a gap whose notehead cannot be placed, or is not exactly one of the poem's words or one whole line with text, as the restore says, or gaps that overlap. [`annotationsOf`](src/practice/editorsNotes.ts) places a step's editor's notes on the poem by their noteheads, as annotations, for the frame and for Form Practice; [`gapsOf`](src/practice/restore.ts) places a restore's gaps, in the poem's order, each with the text it takes out, leaving out one that cannot be placed, and [`slipsOf`](src/practice/restore.ts) puts their texts in alphabetical order, the order the reader is given the slips in, so that their order is the same on every visit and says nothing of where they belong, both for the frame and for Form Practice. A practice's **fingerprint** ([`PracticeFingerprint`](src/practice/types.ts), made by [`fingerprintOfPractice`](src/practice/fingerprint.ts)) is a hash of its stages' steps, written out with their keys sorted, so that any change to a step, its id and its editor's notes included, and nothing else, makes a new one: a new **version** of the practice. The practice's title and its stages' names, ids and days are not hashed: renaming a stage, or changing when it opens, makes no new version. [`samePractice`](src/practice/fingerprint.ts) compares two practices whole, title, names and days included.

A **practice file** in `practice-data/` is a practice as JSON, marked `"format": "anthos-practice"` and `"format_version": 4`: noteheads written in loom's provisional notes grammar. [`loadPractices`](src/practice/load.ts) reads every practice file, and [`parsePractice`](src/practice/load.ts) refuses one this reader does not understand, and [`practiceFileOf`](src/practice/load.ts) writes one, which parsePractice reads back as the same practice; anything read as a practice, from a file or from storage, passes [`isPractice`](src/practice/guard.ts) first, which [`practiceProblem`](src/practice/guard.ts) backs by saying what is wrong and where; a field this reader does not know is refused, since the fingerprint would hash it unseen. [`practiceFor`](src/practice/choose.ts) picks the practice a text runs: the one last saved for it in the browser, or else the first of its practice files, by file name.

### Practice Runner: [`runner/`](src/runner/)

The runner keeps the log, and from it says where the run stands. Its functions are pure, given the time: [`standingOf`](src/runner/step.ts) replays the log into a [`Standing`](src/runner/types.ts):

```ts
export interface Standing {
  readonly station: Station
  readonly opensOn: Day | null
  readonly heldAt: number | null
}
```

The **gate** ([`gateOf`](src/runner/gate.ts)) is the only code that reads the practice at a station. Only the runner adds the rests and the final stage, so a station the practice does not have is the runner's mistake, and it throws. The **law** ([`ruling`](src/runner/step.ts)) says whether the reader may move to a step, and whether the move finishes the stage, or gives a [`Reason`](src/runner/types.ts) why not: no such step, the next stage not open, a step on the way that asks for writing and has none, restores and is not restored, or discloses and is not disclosed (`unanswered`), the reader at a step that restores and is not restored, which they may not leave (`restoring`), or, past the final stage, the practice over. Steps are counted from where the reader stands ([`standsAt`](src/runner/gate.ts)): the station, or, in the final stage, the last stage's rest, so that a step back from the final stage is the last stage's last step. The law knows nothing of how the reader moves; that is the frame's **gait**. A move the law allows is logged by [`eventsOfMove`](src/runner/step.ts). The event that finishes a stage carries the day the next stage opens: the day it was finished, and as many days more as the next stage's days say ([`daysUntilNext`](src/runner/gate.ts), counted with [`daysAfter`](src/clock/clock.ts)), or none before the final stage. So a change to a stage's days, which makes no new version, reaches only a run that has not yet finished the stage before. A finished stage never holds the reader again, and a step is held only until it has a note written on this version of the practice, or, when it restores, until the log has it restored: [`eventOfRestoring`](src/runner/step.ts) logs that, once, at a step that restores; or, when it discloses, until the log has it disclosed: [`eventOfDisclosing`](src/runner/step.ts) logs that, once, at a step that discloses. Only a step that restores keeps the reader from going back. [`isRestoring`](src/runner/step.ts) says, from the log alone, whether the reader is at a step that restores and is not restored, for the Reader, which then keeps them in practice mode. The log is kept per version: [`logKey`](src/runner/log.ts) names the practice, its text and its fingerprint, so an edited practice starts a new run. The runner reads the notebook and never writes it; [`useRun`](src/runner/useRun.ts) runs it in React.

### User Notebook: [`notebook/`](src/notebook/)

The notebook is what the reader writes about a text, one per text, kept for good. It is kept as [`NotebookEvent`](src/notebook/types.ts)s (`noted`, `revised`, `withdrawn`), never overwritten, so no earlier version of a note is lost. Each [`ReaderNote`](src/notebook/types.ts) is an [`Annotation`](src/material/types.ts) of the reader's (its targets, tags and body), with the [`Fingerprint`](src/material/types.ts) of the lines it was written on, and a [`Provenance`](src/notebook/types.ts): the practice, the practice's fingerprint, and the stage and step it was written on, or `null` for a note written outside a practice, as in annotate mode. Its targets are positions in the lines it was written on, so code that places a reader's note on the poem checks its fingerprint first; and its stage and step are places in the version of the practice it was written on, so code that counts or recalls a note from a practice checks the practice's fingerprint first. A note is first written as a [`NewNote`](src/notebook/types.ts), everything but its id and its times; a revision changes its targets and its body, never its fingerprint, so its targets stay positions in the lines it was written on. [`notesOf`](src/notebook/notebook.ts) folds the events into the notes as they stand; [`eventOfNoting`](src/notebook/notebook.ts), [`eventOfRevising`](src/notebook/notebook.ts) and [`eventOfWithdrawing`](src/notebook/notebook.ts) turn the reader's intentions into events, and refuse what makes no sense; [`notesFrom`](src/notebook/notebook.ts) finds the notes written on one step, and [`notesOfStage`](src/notebook/notebook.ts) those written in one stage, each of one version of a practice. A [`NoteId`](src/notebook/types.ts) is made in one place only. [`useNotebook`](src/notebook/useNotebook.ts) opens a text's notebook in React. Only the dev panel forgets a notebook ([`clearNotebook`](src/notebook/log.ts)), in development only.

### Frame: [`reader/frame/`](src/reader/frame/)

The frame sits on one step. [`frameOf`](src/reader/frame/frame.ts) makes a [`Frame`](src/reader/frame/types.ts) from where the run stands: a heading (the stage's name, or the practice's title in the final stage), the law's rulings on a step back and a step on, and the **subject**: the step's editor's notes, placed by their noteheads, as a set of their own, in the step's order, to be disclosed, with whether they are disclosed already, which they are unless the reader is held at the step, and always when there are none; what the reader wrote there, found in the notebook; and the writing's target, resolved against the material: `"whole"` is the [`wholePoem`](src/material/spans.ts); `"chosen"` leaves the targets to the reader, on the material's lines, and only the notes written on those lines whose every target keeps the rule for spans are found there. With `recall`, the subject's commentary is the reader's notes written in the stage it names, on the step it names, or, in the final stage, in every stage before (`all`), on this version of the practice, that can be placed on the poem, in the order of the stages and steps they answered and then the order written, listed, each with the prose of the step it answered (found through the gate) and whether it is on the whole poem, and the first note of each stage with the stage's name as its heading; they can be neither deleted nor edited. With `restore`, the subject's restore is its gaps, placed by their noteheads and in the poem's order, each with the text it takes out ([`gapsOf`](src/practice/restore.ts)); its slips, that same text, in alphabetical order ([`slipsOf`](src/practice/restore.ts)); and whether the step is restored already, which it is unless the reader is held there. The practice and the standing go no further than the frame, except in the law's reasons: a refusal can tell the praxis bar the day the next stage opens. The frame's **gait** is a step back and a step on, from where the reader stands; [`controlsOf`](src/reader/frame/controls.ts) makes those rulings into [`FrameControls`](src/reader/frame/controls.ts), a control that goes saying whether it finishes the stage, and a refusal as `unanswered`, as `restoring` or as the practice over becomes no control at all. On a step of a stage, the frame also gives the step's **position** in its stage (its number, and how many steps the stage has); at a rest, in the final stage and in annotate mode it gives none. In annotate mode, [`annotateFrame`](src/reader/frame/frame.ts) has no rulings; its subject is the notes the reader wrote in annotate mode (those with no provenance), in the order written, shown and listed, with the notes themselves, so that one can be deleted; and **noting** on the material's poem, with no tags. A reader's note is placed on the poem only when it has the poem's fingerprint and every target keeps the rule for spans; the others are left out.

### Scene: [`reader/scenes/`](src/reader/scenes/)

The scene plays a [`Subject`](src/reader/scenes/types.ts). It knows no practice and no station, only parts:

```ts
/** Which step this is. A new key starts the scene afresh. The same key with a fresh subject keeps
 * the scene's state while something around it changes, such as the notebook. */
readonly key: string
```

It has one behaviour per part. The **commentary** ([`parts/commentary.ts`](src/reader/scenes/parts/commentary.ts)) answers the viewer: which note is open ([`OpenNote`](src/reader/scenes/parts/commentary.ts)), from the set the subject shows, followed by its id when the set changes (a note without an id, by its place); and, when the subject carries the reader's own notes, `deleteNote`, which closes the open note and sends out the withdrawal of the note with that id. For notes it recalls, the commentary carries each one's prompt, whether it is on the whole poem, and, on the first of each stage, the stage's name. Notes to disclose (`disclosed`) are shown one by one, and answer the praxis bar, not the viewer: the commentary keeps how many are disclosed, and the open note is the one being read. `disclose` discloses the next note, in the subject's order, and opens it; disclosing the last sends out that the step is disclosed, unless it was disclosed already; `turn` opens a note already disclosed; a tap in the viewer changes nothing. Notes disclosed already are all disclosed from the start, with the first open. The same key with a fresh subject keeps how many are disclosed and the open note; a subject disclosed since, here or in another tab, has every note disclosed, and one no longer disclosed starts afresh. The **writing** ([`parts/writing.ts`](src/reader/scenes/parts/writing.ts)) answers the praxis bar: it keeps the draft and, when its note is on passages the reader chooses, the chosen targets, and acts on `draft`, `submit`, `edit` and `cancel`. Once written, the note is shown; `edit` brings back its words and its targets, and a revision may change either. The **noting** ([`parts/noting.ts`](src/reader/scenes/parts/noting.ts)) is the reader's new note on passages they choose. It keeps whether choosing is on, the chosen targets, and the draft, answers the praxis bar's `noteDraft`, `addNote` and `takeUp`, which takes a note in hand to be written again, its targets and its words, and projects each target's lemma. On `addNote`, while the draft says something, it sends out a new note: on the chosen targets, or the whole poem when none is chosen, with the subject's tags and the draft as its body; then it starts a new note, keeping whether choosing is on. A message from the praxis bar to the noting, and a passage chosen in the viewer for it, also close the open note. The writing, when its note is on passages the reader chooses, and the noting choose passages in the same way ([`passages.ts`](src/reader/scenes/passages.ts)): while choosing is on, a passage chosen in the viewer (`choose`) joins the chosen **targets**, which never overlap and are kept in the poem's order, a passage that overlaps or touches others being joined with them; the praxis bar turns choosing on or off (`choosing`) and removes a target (`unchoose`); and no target chosen means the whole poem. The writing takes these messages while its note is on passages the reader chooses; otherwise the noting does. The **restore** ([`parts/restore.ts`](src/reader/scenes/parts/restore.ts)) keeps which slip fills each gap, and which gaps are restored, which are fixed for good. A slip taken in the praxis bar (`take`) fills the first empty gap, in the poem's order; a filled gap tapped in the viewer (`empty`) gives its slip back; and `check`, only once every gap is filled, restores each gap whose slip's text is the gap's, and gives back every other slip, emptying its gap, so that slips with the same text fit each other's gaps. When every gap is restored it sends out that the step is restored; a subject restored already starts with every gap restored. A new key starts the game afresh. The **picking** ([`parts/picking.ts`](src/reader/scenes/parts/picking.ts)), in Form Practice, makes each of the poem's words, or each of its lines, something to pick, saying of each whether a gap takes it out; it keeps nothing, and a unit picked in the viewer (`pick`) leaves as an effect, its span, for Form Practice to make a gap of it or take the gap away. The prose has no behaviour. The scene also tells the praxis bar what the subject **asks** of the reader besides its prose, from its parts: to write (`write`), to look back at notes recalled (`recall`), to read the editor's notes as they are disclosed (`disclose`), to restore the poem (`restore`), or, with none of these, only to read (`read`); with a noting, as in annotate mode, or a picking, as in Form Practice, it asks nothing (`null`). The scene does no I/O: what the reader writes leaves as an [`Effect`](src/reader/scenes/types.ts), a new annotation (its targets, tags and body), a revision (its targets and body), or a withdrawal, and the mode writes it in the notebook with the fingerprint and where it was written; a step restored leaves as an effect (`restored`), and a step disclosed as another (`disclosed`), which the mode keeps in the run's log; and a word or line picked leaves as an effect (`pick`), which Form Practice makes an edit of. The viewer and the praxis bar never talk to each other. The viewer talks only to the scene; the praxis bar talks to the scene, and moves the run through the frame's controls. [`scene`](src/reader/scenes/scene.ts) is pure; [`useScene`](src/reader/scenes/useScene.ts) keeps it in React, and delivers its effects after keeping its state.

### Viewer: [`reader/viewer/`](src/reader/viewer/)

The viewer draws the scene's [`ViewerProjection`](src/reader/scenes/types.ts), which names the text and carries the set of notes it shows, the editor's or the reader's, with those to pick out (`marked`), list (`listed`) or disclose (`disclosed`), of which it marks only the open one and lets none be tapped, the one that is open, whether the notes shown can be deleted, for recalled notes each one's prompt, whether it is on the whole poem (whose lemma a listed note then leaves out) and its stage's heading, drawn above it, whether choosing is on, and the chosen targets, which it draws; for a restore, its gaps, each empty, filled with a slip's text or restored, and which gap the next slip fills; and, for a picking, each word or line to pick, and whether a gap takes it out, which it draws as a button. It draws each gap in place of the text it takes out, which it never draws until the gap is restored. It takes only the poem from the material. It tells the scene which note was tapped, closed or deleted, which filled gap was tapped, and which word or line was picked. While choosing is on, the reader chooses passages in the viewer, not the browser, and the viewer tells the scene each passage chosen, as a span made by [`trimmedSpan`](src/material/spans.ts); how finely a passage is chosen depends on the reader's pointer ([`choosing.ts`](src/reader/viewer/choosing.ts)). [`PoemBlock`](src/reader/viewer/PoemBlock.tsx) cuts each line at the edges of its notes' ranges ([`cut`](src/reader/viewer/cut.ts)).

### Praxis Bar: [`reader/praxisBar/`](src/reader/praxisBar/)

The praxis bar draws two things that never meet: the frame's controls (its heading, the step's position, Back and Next), and the scene's [`PraxisBarProjection`](src/reader/scenes/types.ts) (what the subject asks; the prose; the writing, in [`NotebookPage`](src/reader/praxisBar/NotebookPage.tsx), with, while a note on passages the reader chooses is written, the toggle for choosing and each chosen target's lemma with a way to remove it ([`ChosenPassages`](src/reader/praxisBar/ChosenPassages.tsx)), and the note's lemma once it is written; and the noting, in [`NotingPage`](src/reader/praxisBar/NotingPage.tsx): the same toggle and targets, the box, and Add note while the box has text; the notes disclosed, in [`DisclosedNotes`](src/reader/praxisBar/DisclosedNotes.tsx), with which is open, whether more are to come, and how many the step has in all, which decides only how they are drawn, never what the reader is told, under the subject's key, so that what it draws starts afresh on another step; and the restore, in [`SlipTray`](src/reader/praxisBar/SlipTray.tsx): the slips, Check while every gap is filled, how many gaps are restored, and how many slips the last check gave back). It tells the scene what the reader writes, which slip they take, and which note to disclose or open, and puts a refusal into words ([`reasonText`](src/reader/praxisBar/reasonText.ts)).

### Form Practice: [`practice-maker/`](src/practice-maker/)

Form Practice makes and changes practices. [`FormPractice`](src/practice-maker/FormPractice.tsx) is a screen of its own, for one text: it loads the text's material, and keeps a draft ([`useDraft`](src/practice-maker/useDraft.ts)): the one kept in the browser for the text, or else the practice the text runs, or else a new practice whose id is made in the hook; opened at its address for a new practice, it starts one, asking first over changes not yet saved. A changed draft is kept in the browser until it is saved, discarded, or changed back to the practice it started from ([`draftToKeep`](src/practice-maker/draft.ts)). A new practice ([`newPractice`](src/practice-maker/draft.ts)) has one stage with one step, and nothing written. A step's prose is written in one box, its paragraphs parted by blank lines ([`paragraphsOf`](src/practice-maker/prose.ts)), and a recall is chosen from the stages and steps it may name there ([`recallTargets`](src/practice-maker/summary.ts)), which are those problemsOf takes. Every change to the draft is an [`Edit`](src/practice-maker/edits.ts), applied by [`edited`](src/practice-maker/edits.ts), a pure function: retitling the practice; adding, renaming, moving and removing stages, and setting their days; adding, moving and removing steps; a step's prose, and its other parts, which are set together, so that a step is never left with two; and adding, changing, moving and removing its editor's notes; and adding and removing a restore's gaps. Each edit names its stage, step and note by place, and an edit naming a place the draft does not have changes nothing; after any change to the stages, the first has no days and every later one has some. A new stage or step is given an id made in the hook: the first of `stage-1`, `stage-2`, … or of `step-1`, `step-2`, … that the draft neither uses nor names in a recall ([`nextStageId`](src/practice-maker/edits.ts), [`nextStepId`](src/practice-maker/edits.ts)), so that a recall left naming one that was removed never comes to name another. Removing a stage or step that a recall names is allowed, and leaves the recall naming nothing, which problemsOf refuses. A new editor's note is written as annotate mode writes a reader's note: [`makerFrame`](src/practice-maker/makerFrame.ts) makes a subject of the step being made, keyed by its id, with its notes picked out and, while the step discloses, a noting; the Scene plays it; and its `note` effect becomes an edit that adds the note ([`noteOf`](src/practice-maker/makerFrame.ts)), its chosen targets written as a notehead by [`noteheadOf`](src/material/noteheads.ts), with an id made in the hook: the first of `note-1`, `note-2`, … the draft does not use ([`nextNoteId`](src/practice-maker/edits.ts)). A note's passages are chosen again by taking it up in the noting (`takeUp`), with its passages and its words; while it is in hand, makerFrame leaves it out of the notes it picks out, and the note the noting then sends out changes it in place, keeping its id, its tags and its place in the step ([`editOfNote`](src/practice-maker/makerFrame.ts)). The step's card lists its notes in the order they are disclosed, each of which moves earlier or later, except while a note is in hand. A restore's gaps are chosen in the poem: while a step that restores is being made, makerFrame gives its subject a picking, of the poem's words or of its lines, as the restore takes out, and the `pick` effect becomes an edit ([`editOfPick`](src/practice-maker/makerFrame.ts)): one that takes the gap away, when a gap of the step is placed on the span picked, or else one that adds a gap there, in the poem's order, its notehead written by [`noteheadOf`](src/material/noteheads.ts). The step's card shows its slips as the reader will be given them, each of which can be taken away; and changing what the restore takes out, words or lines, or choosing another part, while it has gaps, asks first, as the gaps go. [`problemsOf`](src/practice/problems.ts) checks the draft as it changes, and only a draft with no problems is saved: [`eventOfSaving`](src/practice-maker/store.ts) refuses any other, and saving a practice just as it is saved, by [`samePractice`](src/practice/fingerprint.ts), adds nothing. Saved practices are kept per text as [`MadeEvent`](src/practice-maker/store.ts)s (`saved`, `withdrawn`), never overwritten, and read back through [`isMadeEvent`](src/practice-maker/store.ts); [`madePracticesOf`](src/practice-maker/store.ts) folds them into the practices as they stand, in the order last saved, and [`eventOfWithdrawing`](src/practice-maker/store.ts) takes one back, so that its text runs its practice file again; [`useMadePractices`](src/practice-maker/useMadePractices.ts) opens them in React, for Form Practice and for the Reader. A practice saved here keeps its id, so a change to its steps is a new version of it, and a new run. The reel shows the practice's name, written in place ([`PracticeName`](src/practice-maker/PracticeHead.tsx)), and each step by the start of its prose ([`excerptOf`](src/practice-maker/summary.ts)) and its part in a word or two ([`partWord`](src/practice-maker/summary.ts)); a card's tools move what it shows and remove it ([`CardTools`](src/practice-maker/CardTools.tsx)). Form Practice exports a practice as a practice file.

### Around the layers

- **The Reader** ([`reader/`](src/reader/)): the [`Reader`](src/reader/Reader.tsx) for one text loads its material, takes the practice the text runs ([`practiceFor`](src/practice/choose.ts)) from the practice files and the practices saved in the browser ([`useMadePractices`](src/practice-maker/useMadePractices.ts)), and picks a mode ([`modeOf`](src/reader/mode.ts)): the one asked for, except that while the reader is at a step that restores and is not restored ([`isRestoring`](src/runner/step.ts)), annotate mode is not opened, and practice mode is. [`PracticeMode`](src/reader/PracticeMode.tsx) holds the run, the notebook, the frame and the scene, and writes the scene's effects in the notebook; the Reader gives it the practice's fingerprint as its key, so a new version of the practice is a new mode. [`AnnotateMode`](src/reader/AnnotateMode.tsx) holds the notebook, a frame, a scene and the praxis bar, with no runner, and writes the scene's effects in the notebook: a new note with the fingerprint and no provenance, or a withdrawal. The **pen** ([`ModeToggle`](src/reader/ModeToggle.tsx)) switches between them through the URL, and switching brings a new frame and a new scene; at a step that restores and is not restored, it does not switch.
- **The clock** ([`clock/`](src/clock/)) says what time it is and which day. **Storage** ([`storage/`](src/storage/)) keeps the log, the notebook, the practices saved from Form Practice, Form Practice's draft and the reader's choice of light in the browser, and reads each back through a guard, so a damaged log loses only what it cannot read. The draft and the choice of light are the two things it overwrites ([`writeValue`](src/storage/value.ts)); everything else is only ever added to.
- **The library** ([`library/`](src/library/)) lists every text, each a way into its Reader and, in development only, into Form Practice. It is an index of first lines: each poem by its first line, in their order, with its **silhouette**, its lines drawn as bars as long as they are and set in by their indents ([`Silhouette`](src/library/Silhouette.tsx)): [`shapeOf`](src/library/shape.ts) takes a poem's [`Shape`](src/library/shape.ts) from its stanzas, and [`silhouetteOf`](src/library/shape.ts) draws it as [`Bar`](src/library/shape.ts)s in a [`Measure`](src/library/shape.ts), on one scale for the whole library, set by its longest line ([`longestOf`](src/library/shape.ts)), so that shapes compare. [`components/`](src/components/) holds what more than one part draws with: lemmas ([`Lemma`](src/components/Lemma.tsx)), the poem's column with the margin beside it ([`Folio`](src/components/Folio.tsx)), and the controls. The app ([`App.tsx`](src/App.tsx), [`route.ts`](src/route.ts)) opens the library, a Reader, or, in development only, Form Practice, from the URL; outside development, Form Practice's address opens the library. It also keeps the **light** it is all shown in ([`light.ts`](src/light.ts)): the [`Light`](src/light.ts) the reader chose, or, until they choose one, the system's ([`lightIn`](src/light.ts)). It marks the document with a choice, which the theme follows, and the **light toggle** ([`LightToggle`](src/LightToggle.tsx)), in the library, the Reader and Form Practice, makes one. [`assertNever`](src/assertNever.ts) closes every `switch` over a union, and any layer may use it. The **dev panel** ([`reader/dev/`](src/reader/dev/)), in development only, works the runner, the clock and the notebook directly.

## The principle: a step is its parts

> A step is written as its parts, under an id that names it, and is nothing else. The Practice Runner, the Frame, the Scene, the Viewer, the Praxis Bar and Form Practice all work with parts, and no code has a kind of step.

So a new part needs its type in the Practice Reel, its shape in [`practiceProblem`](src/practice/guard.ts), its checks in [`problemsOf`](src/practice/problems.ts), its resolution in the Frame, a behaviour in the Scene, its drawing in the Viewer or the Praxis Bar, and its place in Form Practice. A step has its prose and at most one other part: `problemsOf` refuses more.

## Who may know whom

The table [`LAYERS`](src/test/layers.ts) in [`src/test/layers.ts`](src/test/layers.ts) is the law of who may know whom: each layer may import from itself and from the layers listed for it, and nothing else. Besides the table, any layer may use [`assertNever`](src/assertNever.ts). [`src/test/architecture.test.ts`](src/test/architecture.test.ts) enforces it: it fails on any import the table does not allow, and on any folder of source the table does not name.

```ts
export const LAYERS: Readonly<Record<string, readonly string[]>> = {
  material: [], // Literary Material: knows nothing else
  loom: ["material"], // builds Literary Material from loom's JSON
  practice: ["material"], // Practice Reel
```

In words, the table says:

- **Literary Material** knows nothing; [`loom/`](src/loom/) knows the material.
- The **Practice Reel** knows the material, because its notes are placed on the material's lines.
- The **User Notebook** knows the material, the practice, the clock and storage.
- The **Practice Runner** knows the practice, the notebook, the clock and storage, and may know the material.
- The **Frame** knows the material, the practice, the runner, the notebook, and the Scene's [`Subject`](src/reader/scenes/types.ts): the Scene defines what it receives, and the Frame makes it.
- The **Scene** knows the material and the notebook, never the practice or the runner.
- The **Viewer** knows the material, the Scene and [`components/`](src/components/).
- The **Praxis Bar** knows the Scene, the Frame (for its controls), the runner (for the law's reasons), the clock and [`components/`](src/components/).
- The **Reader** knows every layer but storage and the library. The dev panel knows the practice, the clock, the runner and the notebook. The library knows the material, loom and the app.
- **Form Practice** knows the material, loom, the practice, the clock, storage, the Scene, the Viewer, the Praxis Bar, [`components/`](src/components/) and the app.
- The **Reader** also knows Form Practice, for the practices saved in the browser. The **app** knows the library, the Reader and Form Practice, storage, for the choice of light, and [`components/`](src/components/).

## Where does new code go?

- **About the poem, its positions, spans and noteheads?** [`material/`](src/material/), or [`loom/`](src/loom/) if it reads loom's JSON.
- **Something the editor writes in a practice?** [`practice/`](src/practice/).
- **Making or changing a practice?** [`practice-maker/`](src/practice-maker/).
- **Where a reader is, what they may do next, when a stage opens?** [`runner/`](src/runner/).
- **Something the reader writes, or reads back?** [`notebook/`](src/notebook/).
- **What the Reader does with the reader's actions, with no I/O?** [`reader/scenes/`](src/reader/scenes/), as a part.
- **Drawing?** [`reader/viewer/`](src/reader/viewer/) or [`reader/praxisBar/`](src/reader/praxisBar/), or [`components/`](src/components/) when more than one part draws it; how it looks is free.
- **Wiring hooks, storage and effects together?** The mode: [`PracticeMode`](src/reader/PracticeMode.tsx) or [`AnnotateMode`](src/reader/AnnotateMode.tsx).

If the answer is "several of these", the piece probably needs splitting along the layers.
