# Concept

## Synopsis

A poem arrives in `text-data/` as a loom textual complex. [`loom/`](src/loom/) builds it into **Literary Material**: the poem's lines, each with loom's id for it (its [`Substrate`](src/material/types.ts)), and a [`Poem`](src/material/types.ts) of stanzas and lines. loom's own annotations are not read: the editor's notes are written in the practice.

An editor makes a [`Practice`](src/practice/types.ts) for that text in **Form Practice**: the **Practice Reel**. It is split into [`Stage`](src/practice/types.ts)s, and each stage into [`Step`](src/practice/types.ts)s, and a step is written as its **parts**: words for the praxis bar (`prose`), the editor's notes to pick out in the poem (`highlight`), something for the reader to write (`writing`), and the reader's own notes to bring back (`recall`). Each [`EditorsNote`](src/practice/types.ts) is written in the step that shows it, and placed on the poem by its **notehead**, in loom's notation: `"l4 fellow && l4 raw"`. A practice is kept as a **practice file** in `practice-data/`, or saved in the browser from Form Practice. After each stage comes a **rest**, and after the last stage the **final stage**, which brings back everything the reader wrote in the practice, when it asked for any writing; the editor writes neither.

The **Practice Runner** takes one reader through the practice, over days. What the reader does is kept in a **log** of [`Event`](src/runner/types.ts)s: a step visited, a stage finished. Replaying the log, with the reader's notebook and today's [`Day`](src/clock/clock.ts), gives the **standing** ([`Standing`](src/runner/types.ts)): the **station** (which stage, which step), the day the next stage opens, and the step the reader is **held** at. Reaching a stage's rest finishes it. The first stage is open from the start; each later stage opens on the day after the reader finishes the one before, and the final stage as soon as the last is finished; a day starts at 04:00. A step that asks for writing holds the reader until the **User Notebook** has a note from it, written on this version of the practice. The log is kept per version of the practice: an edited practice is a new run. So the poem is not read all at once: it opens at the reader's pace, a day at a time, and each stage walks the reader through its steps.

On each step, the **Frame** takes the step the runner's **gate** holds ([`gateOf`](src/runner/gate.ts)), places its editor's notes on the poem by their noteheads, resolves what its writing asks to be written about against the poem, and finds what the reader has already written there. That is the **subject**. The **Scene** plays the subject: it keeps what is open and what is being written, and projects it to the **Viewer**, which draws the poem and the notes it picks out, and to the **Praxis Bar**, which draws the prose, the writing, and the frame's Back and Next. What the reader writes leaves the scene as an **effect**, and is kept in the notebook as the reader's own annotation, with where in the run it was written.

The same poem can also be read in **annotate mode**, with no runner: the notes the reader writes there, in the order written, which they can delete, and a note to write on passages they choose.

**Form Practice** is a screen of its own, opened from the library beside each poem. There a practice is built stage by stage and step by step, part by part; each editor's note is written on passages chosen in the poem, as in annotate mode. A practice with no problems is saved in the browser, and can be exported as a practice file.

## Layers

### Literary Material: [`material/`](src/material/)

The material is the text as everything else uses it, and it knows nothing about any of them. [`toMaterial`](src/loom/toMaterial.ts) builds a [`Material`](src/material/materials.ts) from loom's JSON, with [`substrateOf`](src/loom/textualComplex.ts) and [`toPoem`](src/loom/toPoem.ts), and refuses a poem with no text. It does not read loom's annotations.

The **substrate** ([`Substrate`](src/material/types.ts)) is the poem's lines, as loom keeps them, each with loom's id for it (`l4`), and the **fingerprint** ([`Fingerprint`](src/material/types.ts), made by [`fingerprintOf`](src/material/fingerprint.ts)) is a hash of those lines' text, not their ids, made with [`hashOf`](src/material/fingerprint.ts), the hash behind every fingerprint. The [`Poem`](src/material/types.ts) and its [`Line`](src/material/types.ts)s are the lines arranged for display. A text is known by its [`TextId`](src/material/types.ts), the file it comes from.

A **position** ([`Position`](src/material/types.ts)) is a line of the substrate and an offset in it, counted in code points ([`codePoints`](src/material/codePoints.ts)), the unit loom counts in; a space is what loom counts as one ([`isSpace`](src/material/codePoints.ts)). A **span** ([`Span`](src/material/types.ts)) runs from one position up to, not including, another, and follows loom's rule ([`spanProblem`](src/material/spans.ts)): it lies in the poem, runs forwards, never starts at a line's end and never ends at a line's start, so each stretch of text has one span. [`wholePoem`](src/material/spans.ts) is the span from the first line with text to the end of the last; [`sameSpan`](src/material/spans.ts) compares two spans, [`textOf`](src/material/spans.ts) cuts a span's text out of the substrate, and [`lineLength`](src/material/spans.ts) measures a line. [`comparePositions`](src/material/spans.ts) orders two positions; [`trimmedSpan`](src/material/spans.ts) makes a span between two positions that begins and ends on a character that is not a space, so that loom can always write it; [`wordAt`](src/material/spans.ts) is the word (a run of non-space characters) at a position; [`joinSpan`](src/material/spans.ts) adds a span to others, joining it with any it overlaps or touches; [`lineRangesOf`](src/material/spans.ts) gives a span's stretch on each line.

A **notehead** is loom's notation for what a note is about: targets separated by `&&`, each one locator or two joined by `~`, a locator being a line's id, then perhaps a quote from that line, then perhaps `[n]` for the quote's nth occurrence: `"l2 He ~ l3 halves"`, `"l1 ever[2]"`, `"l13"`. [`spansOfNotehead`](src/material/noteheads.ts) places a notehead on the substrate as loom does, or says why it cannot, refusing besides what loom could not read back as a head in a notes file and what breaks the rule for spans; [`noteheadOf`](src/material/noteheads.ts) writes spans as a notehead, so that placing it again gives the same spans. Quotes are matched in NFC and counted in code points, as loom counts them.

An **annotation** ([`Annotation`](src/material/types.ts)) is a note on the poem, as loom has one: an id, its **targets** (spans), its tags and its body. [`notesFor`](src/material/notes.ts) derives how annotations are shown, the editor's or the reader's: each [`Note`](src/material/types.ts) with its id, its lemma ([`lemmaOf`](src/material/notes.ts)) and its paragraphs, and each line's [`NoteRange`](src/material/types.ts)s; a note on the whole poem has none. Its [`Notes`](src/material/types.ts) are what the viewer is given.

### Practice Reel: [`practice/`](src/practice/)

A practice is written before it is run, and does not change while it runs: every field is `readonly`. A [`Step`](src/practice/types.ts) is its [`Parts`](src/practice/types.ts), and nothing reads it any other way:

```ts
export interface Parts {
  readonly prose: readonly string[]
  readonly highlight: readonly EditorsNote[]
  readonly writing: Writing | null
  readonly recall: Recall | null
}
```

An [`EditorsNote`](src/practice/types.ts) is a note as loom has one, written in the step that shows it: an id (letters, digits, `-` and `_`, as loom allows), a notehead, tags and a body. The rest and the final stage are written in the same terms, as [`REST`](src/practice/parts.ts) and [`finalPartsOf`](src/practice/parts.ts). A recall brings back the stage before (`previous`), or every stage before (`all`), which in the final stage is the whole practice: the final stage recalls `all` when any step of the practice asks for writing, and nothing otherwise. [`problemsOf`](src/practice/problems.ts) checks a practice against its text's substrate before it runs, and refuses: no stages; a stage with no steps; a step that says nothing and asks nothing; a note id that is malformed, or repeated in the practice; a notehead that cannot be placed; a step that both highlights and recalls, since a subject has one commentary; and a recall with nothing it could bring back: in the first stage, which has no stage before it, or when none of the stages it recalls from (the stage before, or every stage before) asks for writing. [`annotationsOf`](src/practice/editorsNotes.ts) places a step's editor's notes on the poem by their noteheads, as annotations, for the frame and for Form Practice. A practice's **fingerprint** ([`PracticeFingerprint`](src/practice/types.ts), made by [`fingerprintOfPractice`](src/practice/fingerprint.ts)) is a hash of its stages' steps, written out with their keys sorted, so that any change to a step, its editor's notes included, and nothing else, makes a new one: a new **version** of the practice.

A **practice file** in `practice-data/` is a practice as JSON, marked `"format": "anthos-practice"` and `"format_version": 1`: noteheads written in loom's provisional notes grammar. [`loadPractices`](src/practice/load.ts) reads every practice file, and [`parsePractice`](src/practice/load.ts) refuses one this reader does not understand; anything read as a practice, from a file or from storage, passes [`isPractice`](src/practice/guard.ts) first, which [`practiceProblem`](src/practice/guard.ts) backs by saying what is wrong and where; a field this reader does not know is refused, since the fingerprint would hash it unseen. [`practiceFor`](src/practice/choose.ts) picks the practice a text runs: the one last saved for it in the browser, or else the first of its practice files, by file name.

### Practice Runner: [`runner/`](src/runner/)

The runner keeps the log, and from it says where the run stands. Its functions are pure, given the time: [`standingOf`](src/runner/step.ts) replays the log into a [`Standing`](src/runner/types.ts):

```ts
export interface Standing {
  readonly station: Station
  readonly opensOn: Day | null
  readonly heldAt: number | null
}
```

The **gate** ([`gateOf`](src/runner/gate.ts)) is the only code that reads the practice at a station. Only the runner adds the rests and the final stage, so a station the practice does not have is the runner's mistake, and it throws. The **law** ([`ruling`](src/runner/step.ts)) says whether the reader may move to a step, or gives a [`Reason`](src/runner/types.ts) why not: no such step, the next stage not open, a step on the way that asks for writing and has none (`unanswered`), or the practice over. The law knows nothing of how the reader moves; that is the frame's **gait**. A move the law allows is logged by [`eventsOfMove`](src/runner/step.ts). A finished stage never holds the reader again, and a step is held only until it has a note written on this version of the practice. The log is kept per version: [`logKey`](src/runner/log.ts) names the practice, its text and its fingerprint, so an edited practice starts a new run. The runner reads the notebook and never writes it; [`useRun`](src/runner/useRun.ts) runs it in React.

### User Notebook: [`notebook/`](src/notebook/)

The notebook is what the reader writes about a text, one per text, kept for good. It is kept as [`NotebookEvent`](src/notebook/types.ts)s (`noted`, `revised`, `withdrawn`), never overwritten, so no earlier version of a note is lost. Each [`ReaderNote`](src/notebook/types.ts) is an [`Annotation`](src/material/types.ts) of the reader's (its targets, tags and body), with the [`Fingerprint`](src/material/types.ts) of the lines it was written on, and a [`Provenance`](src/notebook/types.ts): the practice, the practice's fingerprint, and the stage and step it was written on, or `null` for a note written outside a practice, as in annotate mode. Its targets are positions in the lines it was written on, so code that places a reader's note on the poem checks its fingerprint first; and its stage and step are places in the version of the practice it was written on, so code that counts or recalls a note from a practice checks the practice's fingerprint first. A note is first written as a [`NewNote`](src/notebook/types.ts), everything but its id and its times; a revision changes its targets and its body, never its fingerprint, so its targets stay positions in the lines it was written on. [`notesOf`](src/notebook/notebook.ts) folds the events into the notes as they stand; [`eventOfNoting`](src/notebook/notebook.ts), [`eventOfRevising`](src/notebook/notebook.ts) and [`eventOfWithdrawing`](src/notebook/notebook.ts) turn the reader's intentions into events, and refuse what makes no sense; [`notesFrom`](src/notebook/notebook.ts) finds the notes written on one step, and [`notesOfStage`](src/notebook/notebook.ts) those written in one stage, each of one version of a practice. A [`NoteId`](src/notebook/types.ts) is made in one place only. [`useNotebook`](src/notebook/useNotebook.ts) opens a text's notebook in React. Only the dev panel forgets a notebook ([`clearNotebook`](src/notebook/log.ts)), in development only.

### Frame: [`reader/frame/`](src/reader/frame/)

The frame sits on one step. [`frameOf`](src/reader/frame/frame.ts) makes a [`Frame`](src/reader/frame/types.ts) from where the run stands: a heading (the stage's name, or the practice's title in the final stage), the law's rulings on a step back and a step on, and the **subject**: the step's editor's notes, placed by their noteheads, as a set of their own, each picked out; what the reader wrote there, found in the notebook; and the writing's target, resolved against the material: `"whole"` is the [`wholePoem`](src/material/spans.ts); `"chosen"` leaves the targets to the reader, on the material's lines, and only the notes written on those lines whose every target keeps the rule for spans are found there. With `recall`, the subject's commentary is the reader's notes written in the stage before (`previous`) or in every stage before (`all`), on this version of the practice, that can be placed on the poem, in the order of the stages and steps they answered and then the order written, listed, each with the prose of the step it answered (found through the gate) and whether it is on the whole poem, and the first note of each stage with the stage's name as its heading; they can be neither deleted nor edited. The practice and the standing go no further than the frame, except in the law's reasons: a refusal can tell the praxis bar the day the next stage opens. The frame's **gait** is a step back and a step on; [`controlsOf`](src/reader/frame/controls.ts) makes those rulings into [`FrameControls`](src/reader/frame/controls.ts), and a refusal as `unanswered` becomes no control at all. In annotate mode, [`annotateFrame`](src/reader/frame/frame.ts) has no rulings; its subject is the notes the reader wrote in annotate mode (those with no provenance), in the order written, shown and listed, with the notes themselves, so that one can be deleted; and **noting** on the material's poem, with no tags. A reader's note is placed on the poem only when it has the poem's fingerprint and every target keeps the rule for spans; the others are left out.

### Scene: [`reader/scenes/`](src/reader/scenes/)

The scene plays a [`Subject`](src/reader/scenes/types.ts). It knows no practice and no station, only parts:

```ts
/** Which step this is. A new key starts the scene afresh. The same key with a fresh subject keeps
 * the scene's state while something around it changes, such as the notebook. */
readonly key: string
```

It has one behaviour per part. The **commentary** ([`parts/commentary.ts`](src/reader/scenes/parts/commentary.ts)) answers the viewer: which note is open ([`OpenNote`](src/reader/scenes/parts/commentary.ts)), from the set the subject shows, followed by its id when the set changes (a note without an id, by its place); and, when the subject carries the reader's own notes, `deleteNote`, which closes the open note and sends out the withdrawal of the note with that id. For notes it recalls, the commentary carries each one's prompt, whether it is on the whole poem, and, on the first of each stage, the stage's name. The **writing** ([`parts/writing.ts`](src/reader/scenes/parts/writing.ts)) answers the praxis bar: it keeps the draft and, when its note is on passages the reader chooses, the chosen targets, and acts on `draft`, `submit`, `edit` and `cancel`. Once written, the note is shown; `edit` brings back its words and its targets, and a revision may change either. The **noting** ([`parts/noting.ts`](src/reader/scenes/parts/noting.ts)) is the reader's new note on passages they choose. It keeps whether choosing is on, the chosen targets, and the draft, answers the praxis bar's `noteDraft` and `addNote`, and projects each target's lemma. On `addNote`, while the draft says something, it sends out a new note: on the chosen targets, or the whole poem when none is chosen, with the subject's tags and the draft as its body; then it starts a new note, keeping whether choosing is on. A message from the praxis bar to the noting, and a passage chosen in the viewer for it, also close the open note. The writing, when its note is on passages the reader chooses, and the noting choose passages in the same way ([`passages.ts`](src/reader/scenes/passages.ts)): while choosing is on, a passage chosen in the viewer (`choose`) joins the chosen **targets**, which never overlap and are kept in the poem's order, a passage that overlaps or touches others being joined with them; the praxis bar turns choosing on or off (`choosing`) and removes a target (`unchoose`); and no target chosen means the whole poem. The writing takes these messages while its note is on passages the reader chooses; otherwise the noting does. The prose has no behaviour. The scene does no I/O: what the reader writes leaves as an [`Effect`](src/reader/scenes/types.ts), a new annotation (its targets, tags and body), a revision (its targets and body), or a withdrawal, and the mode writes it in the notebook with the fingerprint and where it was written. The viewer and the praxis bar never talk to each other. The viewer talks only to the scene; the praxis bar talks to the scene, and moves the run through the frame's controls. [`scene`](src/reader/scenes/scene.ts) is pure; [`useScene`](src/reader/scenes/useScene.ts) keeps it in React, and delivers its effects after keeping its state.

### Viewer: [`reader/viewer/`](src/reader/viewer/)

The viewer draws the scene's [`ViewerProjection`](src/reader/scenes/types.ts), which names the text and carries the set of notes it shows, the editor's or the reader's, with those to pick out (`marked`) or list (`listed`), the one that is open, whether the notes shown can be deleted, for recalled notes each one's prompt, whether it is on the whole poem (whose lemma a listed note then leaves out) and its stage's heading, drawn above it, whether choosing is on, and the chosen targets, which it draws. It takes only the poem from the material. It tells the scene which note was tapped, closed or deleted. While choosing is on, the reader chooses passages in the viewer, not the browser, and the viewer tells the scene each passage chosen, as a span made by [`trimmedSpan`](src/material/spans.ts); how finely a passage is chosen depends on the reader's pointer ([`choosing.ts`](src/reader/viewer/choosing.ts)). [`PoemBlock`](src/reader/viewer/PoemBlock.tsx) cuts each line at the edges of its notes' ranges ([`cut`](src/reader/viewer/cut.ts)).

### Praxis Bar: [`reader/praxisBar/`](src/reader/praxisBar/)

The praxis bar draws two things that never meet: the frame's controls (its heading, Back and Next), and the scene's [`PraxisBarProjection`](src/reader/scenes/types.ts) (the prose; the writing, in [`NotebookPage`](src/reader/praxisBar/NotebookPage.tsx), with, while a note on passages the reader chooses is written, the toggle for choosing and each chosen target's lemma with a way to remove it ([`ChosenPassages`](src/reader/praxisBar/ChosenPassages.tsx)), and the note's lemma once it is written; and the noting, in [`NotingPage`](src/reader/praxisBar/NotingPage.tsx): the same toggle and targets, the box, and Add note while the box has text). It tells the scene what the reader writes, and puts a refusal into words ([`reasonText`](src/reader/praxisBar/reasonText.ts)).

### Form Practice: [`practice-maker/`](src/practice-maker/)

Form Practice makes and changes practices. [`FormPractice`](src/practice-maker/FormPractice.tsx) is a screen of its own, for one text: it loads the text's material, starts from the practice the text runs, or from none, a new practice whose id is made in the hook, and keeps a draft. Every change to the draft is an [`Edit`](src/practice-maker/edits.ts), applied by [`edited`](src/practice-maker/edits.ts), a pure function: retitling the practice; adding, renaming, moving and removing stages; adding, moving and removing steps; a step's prose, writing and recall; and adding, changing, moving and removing its editor's notes. Each edit names its stage, step and note by place, and an edit naming a place the draft does not have changes nothing. A new editor's note is written as annotate mode writes a reader's note: [`makerFrame`](src/practice-maker/makerFrame.ts) makes a subject of the step being made, with its notes picked out and a noting; the Scene plays it; and its `note` effect becomes an edit that adds the note ([`noteOf`](src/practice-maker/makerFrame.ts)), its chosen targets written as a notehead by [`noteheadOf`](src/material/noteheads.ts), with an id made in the hook: the first of `note-1`, `note-2`, … the draft does not use ([`nextNoteId`](src/practice-maker/edits.ts)). [`problemsOf`](src/practice/problems.ts) checks the draft as it changes, and only a draft with no problems is saved: [`eventOfSaving`](src/practice-maker/store.ts) refuses any other, and saving a practice just as it is saved adds nothing. Saved practices are kept per text as [`MadeEvent`](src/practice-maker/store.ts)s (`saved`, `withdrawn`), never overwritten, and read back through [`isMadeEvent`](src/practice-maker/store.ts); [`madePracticesOf`](src/practice-maker/store.ts) folds them into the practices as they stand, in the order last saved, and [`eventOfWithdrawing`](src/practice-maker/store.ts) takes one back, so that its text runs its practice file again; [`useMadePractices`](src/practice-maker/useMadePractices.ts) opens them in React, for Form Practice and for the Reader. A practice saved here keeps its id, so a change is a new version of it, and a new run. Form Practice exports a practice as a practice file.

### Around the layers

- **The Reader** ([`reader/`](src/reader/)): the [`Reader`](src/reader/Reader.tsx) for one text loads its material, takes the practice the text runs ([`practiceFor`](src/practice/choose.ts)) from the practice files and the practices saved in the browser ([`useMadePractices`](src/practice-maker/useMadePractices.ts)), and picks a mode ([`modeOf`](src/reader/mode.ts)). [`PracticeMode`](src/reader/PracticeMode.tsx) holds the run, the notebook, the frame and the scene, and writes the scene's effects in the notebook; the Reader gives it the practice's fingerprint as its key, so a new version of the practice is a new mode. [`AnnotateMode`](src/reader/AnnotateMode.tsx) holds the notebook, a frame, a scene and the praxis bar, with no runner, and writes the scene's effects in the notebook: a new note with the fingerprint and no provenance, or a withdrawal. The **pen** ([`ModeToggle`](src/reader/ModeToggle.tsx)) switches between them through the URL, and switching brings a new frame and a new scene.
- **The clock** ([`clock/`](src/clock/)) says what time it is and which day. **Storage** ([`storage/`](src/storage/)) keeps the log, the notebook and the practices saved from Form Practice in the browser, and reads each back through a guard, so a damaged log loses only what it cannot read.
- **The library** ([`library/`](src/library/)) lists every text, each a way into its Reader and into Form Practice. [`ui/`](src/ui/) holds what every part draws with, lemmas included ([`Lemma`](src/ui/Lemma.tsx)). The app ([`App.tsx`](src/App.tsx), [`route.ts`](src/route.ts)) opens the library, a Reader, or Form Practice from the URL. [`assertNever`](src/assertNever.ts) closes every `switch` over a union, and any layer may use it. The **dev panel** ([`reader/dev/`](src/reader/dev/)), in development only, works the runner, the clock and the notebook directly.

## The principle: a step is its parts

> A step is written as its parts, and is nothing else. The Practice Runner, the Frame, the Scene, the Viewer, the Praxis Bar and Form Practice all work with parts, and no code has a kind of step.

So a new part needs its type in the Practice Reel, its shape in [`practiceProblem`](src/practice/guard.ts), its checks in [`problemsOf`](src/practice/problems.ts), its resolution in the Frame, a behaviour in the Scene, its drawing in the Viewer or the Praxis Bar, and its place in Form Practice. Parts that cannot share a step are refused by `problemsOf`.

## Who may know whom

The table [`LAYERS`](src/test/layers.ts) in [`src/test/layers.ts`](src/test/layers.ts) is the law of who may know whom: each layer may import from itself and from the layers listed for it, and nothing else. Besides the table, any layer may use [`assertNever`](src/assertNever.ts). [`src/test/architecture.test.ts`](src/test/architecture.test.ts) enforces it: it fails on any import the table does not allow, and on any folder of source the table does not name.

```ts
export const LAYERS: Readonly<Record<string, readonly string[]>> = {
  material: [], // Literary Material: knows nothing else
  loom: ["material"], // builds Literary Material from loom's JSON
  practice: ["material"], // Practice Reel
```

In words, the table says:

- **Literary Material** knows nothing; [`loom/`](src/loom/) knows the material.
- The **Practice Reel** knows the material, because its notes are placed on the material's lines.
- The **User Notebook** knows the material, the practice, the clock and storage.
- The **Practice Runner** knows the practice, the notebook, the clock and storage, and may know the material.
- The **Frame** knows the material, the practice, the runner, the notebook, and the Scene's [`Subject`](src/reader/scenes/types.ts): the Scene defines what it receives, and the Frame makes it.
- The **Scene** knows the material and the notebook, never the practice or the runner.
- The **Viewer** knows the material, the Scene and [`ui/`](src/ui/).
- The **Praxis Bar** knows the Scene, the Frame (for its controls), the runner (for the law's reasons), the clock and [`ui/`](src/ui/).
- The **Reader** knows every layer but storage and the library. The dev panel knows the practice, the clock, the runner and the notebook. The library knows loom and the app.
- **Form Practice** knows the material, loom, the practice, the clock, storage, the Scene, the Viewer, the Praxis Bar, [`ui/`](src/ui/) and the app.
- The **Reader** also knows Form Practice, for the practices saved in the browser. The **app** knows the library, the Reader and Form Practice.

A table entry allows an import without requiring it. Where these words and the table disagree, the table is right. Change [`LAYERS`](src/test/layers.ts) only when the layers themselves change, and change this file with it.

## Where does new code go?

- **About the poem, its positions, spans and noteheads?** [`material/`](src/material/), or [`loom/`](src/loom/) if it reads loom's JSON.
- **Something the editor writes in a practice?** [`practice/`](src/practice/).
- **Making or changing a practice?** [`practice-maker/`](src/practice-maker/).
- **Where a reader is, what they may do next, when a stage opens?** [`runner/`](src/runner/).
- **Something the reader writes, or reads back?** [`notebook/`](src/notebook/).
- **What the Reader does with the reader's actions, with no I/O?** [`reader/scenes/`](src/reader/scenes/), as a part.
- **Drawing?** [`reader/viewer/`](src/reader/viewer/) or [`reader/praxisBar/`](src/reader/praxisBar/); how it looks is free.
- **Wiring hooks, storage and effects together?** The mode: [`PracticeMode`](src/reader/PracticeMode.tsx) or [`AnnotateMode`](src/reader/AnnotateMode.tsx).

If the answer is "several of these", the piece probably needs splitting along the layers.

## Retired words

Don't use: _footage_ (the poem), _commentary track_ (the editor's notes), _timecode_ (a position), _anchor_ (a note's targets), _edition_ for the fingerprint (the word is kept for what is made for readers), _frame_ for a step (a step), _intermission_ and _end card_ (the rest, the final stage), _direction_, and _reflect_, _consider_, _respond_ and _observe_ as kinds of step (a step is its parts), _lexicon_, _reference_ and _citation_ for a step's naming of a note (an editor's note is written in its step), _projector_ and _projectionist_ (the runner), _counter_ (station), _read-out_ (standing), _cut_ and _the shot continues_ (a new key, the same key), _picture_ (the viewer), _intertitle_ (prose), _notebook page_ (writing), _notebook entry_ (effect), _projector's controls_ and _booth_ (the frame's controls), _screen_, _screening_ and _viewing table_ (the Reader, practice mode, annotate mode), _programme_ (the library), _print_, _lab_, _claw_, _iris_, _spectator_, _staging_, _shelf_, _house clock_, _tape_, _camera on a film set_.
